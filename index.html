The Problem

Your sample was perfect. The bulk wasn't.

If that story sounds familiar, you already know the problem with buying from ten thousand kilometres away: samples go missing, production slips, and when something goes wrong, nobody on the ground is protecting your side of the deal.

01

Samples go missing

Parcels stall, specs get lost in translation, and weeks pass before you learn a sample was never even made.

02

Production slips — silently

The delay exists long before you hear about it. Too often, you hear about it on the delivery date.

03

The bulk doesn't match

Different fabric, weaker stitching, wrong packing. What ships is not what you approved — and it's already on the water.

04

Nobody is on your side

The factory has its people. The trading company has its margin. From another continent, you have no one whose only job is your interest.

That's the gap I stand in. Let me be clear about one thing: I work for the buyer — never the factory. My only client in your deal is you.

The Promise — How the Money Works

I work for the buyer. Never the factory.

The fastest way to lose money sourcing overseas is paying a middleman for the goods. So here is my whole model, in the open — the same terms stated in every agreement I sign.

You pay the factory directly

You contract with the factory and pay by Letter of Credit (LC) or bank transfer (TT). Your goods money never passes through me — not for one hour, not for one order.

My fee is separate — and in writing

Commission, retainer or fixed fee: agreed in writing before work begins and invoiced to you separately via Payoneer. No surprises on either side.

Nothing from factories. Ever.

I take no supplier-side commission, no kickback, no hidden markup inside your price. If a factory offers me money to push its goods, I refuse it — and I tell you.

Agent, not exporter of record

Your purchase contract is with the Bangladeshi supplier, who manufactures and exports your goods. My role is to find, verify, negotiate, monitor and inspect on your behalf.

If the inspection fails, the goods don't ship

I push the factory to fix or remake them, and you receive photo evidence of everything. I don't disappear when things go wrong — that's exactly when you need me most.

Your business stays yours

Your designs, your prices, your supplier list — I never share one buyer's business with another factory. Confidentiality is part of the job, not an extra.

Honest limits, stated plainly

If Bangladesh is wrong for your product, I'll tell you that too — I'd rather lose one order than your trust. And no honest agent can guarantee a supplier's performance; what I guarantee is process: verified suppliers, direct payment, evidence-based inspection, and immediate, honest communication.

Services

Everything handled on the ground.

Eight services, one job: making sure what ships is what you approved. Each one is available on its own, or together under commission or a monthly retainer.

1

Supplier Discovery & Verification

I find makers who can actually produce your product at your quantity — then verify them before you commit: trade licence and export registration (ERC) checked, premises visited, production capacity confirmed, certifications copied with their scope and dates (not brochure claims), and past export experience checked. You know the factory's real name and location before any deposit is discussed.

2

Factory Audit Visits

I visit the factory in person and send you a written audit report with dated photos and video: the production floor, the workers, the storage, the packing area. Not a forwarded certificate — evidence.

3

Sample Collection & Shipping

Samples arranged from one or more verified makers, checked by me against your specification before dispatch, consolidated into one parcel and couriered to you with honest notes on quality and any differences from your brief.

4

Price Negotiation

You receive the factory's price, negotiated by someone who knows local costs — not a price with a hidden margin inside it. Quotes state clearly what is included and how long they are valid.

5

Production Follow-Up

A written production calendar with milestones, and weekly photo/video updates from the floor. If something slips, you hear it from me the day I know — not on the delivery date.

6

Pre-Shipment Inspection

A final inspection on every order before it ships: quantity, dimensions, workmanship, colour, labelling and packing checked against your approved sample and specification, using AQL sampling. You receive the report with photos and video before the goods leave — and the balance is only due when you're satisfied the shipment matches what you approved.

7

Export-Document Checking

I check the paperwork as it's issued — commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, bill of lading — against your order, and flag discrepancies the same day, while they can still be fixed.

8

Trade-Fair Representation

Can't fly to Dhaka for a sourcing fair? I attend on your behalf: I visit the exhibitors you name (and the ones you should know about), collect catalogues and price lists, take photos and video, and report back with a shortlist and my honest assessment. Quoted in writing per assignment.

Why Bangladesh

The numbers work in your favour.

Bangladesh is the world's second-largest garment exporter and the world's largest jute exporter. But the real argument is arithmetic — duty and labour maths that lands directly on your margin.

0%
EU import duty

Bangladeshi goods enter the EU duty-free and quota-free under Everything But Arms (EBA).

0%
UK import duty

0% under the UK's Developing Countries Trading Scheme (Comprehensive preferences cover 99.8% of goods).

~12%
The China comparison

Comparable apparel shipped from China into the EU typically faces duty of around 12%.

~$113
Monthly garment minimum wage

Approx. US$113/month in Bangladesh — against roughly $200 in Vietnam and $350+ in China (approx.).

#1
World's largest jute exporter

The "golden fibre" — strong, biodegradable, and made into bags, baskets, rugs and décor by SME makers.

Proven
Quality at world scale

Bangladeshi factories already produce for the world's biggest fashion brands, in the world's largest cluster of green-certified garment factories.

And the advantage is not about to expire: Bangladesh's LDC graduation has been postponed to November 2029, and EU EBA access continues through the transition period to around 2032.

Lower cost. Zero duty. Proven quality.

That's margin, protected. The catch? You need someone on the ground to make sure the bulk matches the sample. That's my job.

FAQ

Straight answers.

The questions every careful buyer asks before working with an agent overseas — answered the same way here as in my agreements.

Do I pay you for the goods?
No — never. You contract with the factory and pay the factory directly by Letter of Credit (LC) or bank transfer (TT). My fee covers verification, negotiation, follow-up and inspection only, and is invoiced to you separately via Payoneer.
Are you the exporter of my goods?
No. I am your agent, not the seller or exporter of record. The Bangladeshi supplier manufactures and exports your goods; I find, verify, negotiate, monitor and inspect on your behalf.
When is your fee agreed — and how is it paid?
Before any work begins, in writing: commission on order value, a monthly retainer, or a fixed price per task. Fees are invoiced via Payoneer, a verified payout path to my Bangladeshi bank account. I do not use PayPal.
What happens if the inspection fails?
The goods don't ship. I push the factory to fix or remake them, and you receive photo evidence of everything. Your balance is only due when you're satisfied the shipment matches what you approved — that is the point of inspecting before shipment, not after.
What's the difference between the free shortlist and the $120 shortlist?
The free first shortlist covers one product and returns 3 verified suppliers with prices and minimum quantities — enough to judge whether Bangladesh works for you. The $120 shortlist goes deeper: 5–10 verified suppliers with factory background, capacity, certifications, MOQ, sample cost and my written assessment of each.
Can I start with a small trial order?
Yes — that's exactly how I recommend starting. A trial order proves the factory and proves me, at low risk, before you scale. Small trials are normal with the SME makers I work with in jute and handicrafts; home textiles carry larger minimums.
How fast do you respond — and how do I follow my order?
I reply within one business day (same business day on the Growth retainer). During production you receive weekly photo/video updates against a written calendar, and if something slips, you hear it from me the day I know — not on the delivery date.
Which countries do you work with?
Buyers worldwide. My first markets are the EU and the US, and I regularly work across time zones — I'm in Chattogram (UTC+6), and because my reporting is written, with dated photos and video, time zones never block an order.
Can I see a sample report before paying anything?
Yes. Ask me for a sample inspection report and I'll show you exactly what lands in your inbox on every order — the format, the photos, the checks — before you commit to any paid work.
Do you take commissions from factories?
Never. No supplier-side commission, no kickbacks, no hidden markup. If a factory ever offers me money to push its goods, I decline it and tell you. My only client in your deal is you.
What do you need from me to start?
One message is enough: the product (a link or a photo), your quantity, target price, destination country and timeline. From that, I return verified supplier options — starting with the free 3-supplier shortlist if it's your first product with me.
